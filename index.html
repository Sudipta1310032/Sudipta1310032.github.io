Computational materials scientist

Sudipta Paul

Ph.D. Candidate, Materials Science & Engineering
University of Wisconsin-Madison

I develop machine-learning interatomic potentials and use density functional theory, ab initio molecular dynamics, and large-scale atomistic simulations to study energy materials. My current work focuses on molten salts for nuclear-energy systems and radiation damage in high-entropy ceramics.

I am particularly interested in linking atomic-scale structure and physics to measurable thermophysical properties, defect evolution, and materials performance through reproducible scientific-computing workflows.

Research Experience

Machine-learning interatomic potentials for molten salts

DOE NEUP grant #00011242

January 2023 - July 2026
  • Developed an atomic cluster expansion potential trained on DFT data for composition-wide simulations of binary and ternary molten-salt systems.
  • Generated thermophysical-property data and connected local structural features to behavior relevant to molten-salt reactors.
  • Studied transmutation-product effects using experimental evidence, semi-empirical models, AIMD, and ML-driven molecular dynamics.

Radiation effects in high-entropy carbides

DOE grant #DE-FG02-08ER46493

April 2025 - Present
  • Developed machine-learning interatomic potentials for (NbTiVMo)C4 and (NbTiVZr)C4 high-entropy carbide systems.
  • Performed ZBL-augmented PKA cascade simulations to quantify defect evolution and irradiation-driven microstructural change.
  • Investigated electron-phonon effects and predicted ceramic phase stability using free-energy calculations from AIMD.

Thin-film evaporation and nanoscale condensation
